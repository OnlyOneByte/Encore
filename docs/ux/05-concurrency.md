### Negative findings — what was searched for and not found

- **No published standard, guideline or measured study governs turn-taking fairness, skip authority
  or contention resolution in a co-located shared-queue social product.** `encore-ux-frame.md` §4
  reaches the same conclusion from the other direction, citing `doctrine/concurrency.md`: the
  fairness literature is "candid that mechanism effects on satisfaction are under-studied". Every
  authority row below is therefore `UNSOURCED` by necessity, not by omission. Round-robin is
  recorded in the frame as _a decision to test at a real party_, not a proven-correct choice.
- **No source anywhere prescribes a latency budget for a phone-to-shared-display handoff.** Any
  number in this area is a **choice**. None is invented here.
- **What IS citable** is the _disclosure_ half: WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages, 3.3.1 Error
  Identification and 1.4.1 Use of Color all bind on how a concurrency outcome is communicated, even
  though nothing binds on what the outcome should be. Those rows carry real URLs.

### Reorder contention: last-write-wins is not a refusal

Cross-singer reorder contention resolves **last-write-wins, no toast** (conductor-relayed owner ruling
2026-09-09). When two guests reorder at once, the later write lands and the final order reflects the
last writer. A reorder that loses the race is **superseded, not rejected**: there is no `op:reject` for
reorder contention, so nothing is refused and nothing is announced. Because `XR-CON-13` governs
**refusals** of a guest's action, it does not apply here — a superseded reorder is not a refusal, so it
is not a carve-out from that rule and nothing is exempted from it. **Every actual `op:reject`
disclosure is unchanged** — an `add` over a limit, a `process` over `MAX_CONCURRENT_PROCESSES_PER_SINGER`, and
any other refusal a guest caused are still surfaced on that guest's phone in the room's words.

## (a) Requirements deliberately NOT written, and why

1. **Round-robin ordering fairness.** Stated _and_ enforced: `src/lib/contract/queue/rotation.ts`
   (`storedOrder`, `assignSeqs`, `insertEntry`) with the insert-position rules in
   `src/lib/contract/queue/queue-order.ts`, and a test named literally _"round-robin: everyone's 1st
   pick before anyone's 2nd"_. Restating it would pad the document with a rule that was never at risk.
2. **Self-only removal and within-singer reorder.** Enforced server-side —
   `server/realtime/queue-own-entry-ownership.test.ts`, and `hub.ts:240` records the deliberate
   subtlety that `can()` is consulted **only** for someone else's entry so a narrowing never blocks a
   guest from their own song. Referenced by `XR-CON-04`, not rewritten.
3. **Host-only media deletion.** Enforced at `routes/api/delete-media/+server.ts`, which already
   carries its own migration note (_"swap the joined-singer check for `can(actor, 'admin:delete')`
   once a host role exists"_). It is the same open question as `XR-CON-03` with an existing plan;
   a separate row would duplicate it.
4. **A specific skip-authority policy, a specific rate limit, a specific latency budget, a specific
   contention rule.** No source supports any of them and inventing one would present a choice as a
   finding. They are named-decision rows above and enumerated options below.
5. **Multi-room concurrency.** `server/realtime/room-scope.ts` already centralises tenancy _"like
   `can()` for capability"_, and `isForeignRoom` drops foreign frames at `server.ts:320`.
   The product's concurrency problem is many guests in **one** room; a second room is a different
   spec.
6. **Departure settling.** `server/singers/leave.ts` already routes one `remove` op per entry through
   the authoritative path _"so every client converges the same way it would on a hand-tapped remove"_,
   special-cases the playing entry via `keptPlaying`, and exposes `removableOnLeave` explicitly _"so
   the phone can name the cost before asking"_. Stated, enforced, and better than a new row.
7. **Idempotency and reconnect resend.** `an earlier change`'s ledger-before-reject rule and `resendPending` are
   both implemented and carry unusually complete rationale, including the non-obvious requirement to
   use the non-recording `hasOp` rather than `seenOp`. Nothing here is at risk.
8. **Presence grace windows.** `DEFAULT/MIN/MAX_PRESENCE_GRACE_MINUTES` exist in
   `src/lib/contract/queue/membership.ts` and `refreshMembership()` is called on every queue command.
   Settled.
9. **A TV input authority rule.** The TV has no input device, so "who may act from the TV" is not a
   question the product has. Recorded so the next reader does not add a row for symmetry.
10. **A vote mechanism for skip.** `encore-ux-frame.md` §4 lists "or a vote" as one possibility. It
    is a **feature**, not an authority policy — it needs a UI, a quorum rule and a tie rule, none of
    which exist. It appears as option **C** under Q1 rather than as a requirement.

## (b) OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER — RESOLVED 2026-09-09

**All five are now ruled.** See § "Owner rulings — CONFIRMED 2026-09-09" above for the statement of
record; the outcome was **the status-quo option (A) for every question** (Q1-A, Q2-A, Q3-A, Q4-A
keep-open with pinning removed, Q5-A). The options below are retained as the decision context that
produced those rulings — they are no longer open.

### Q1 — Who may skip the song that is playing?

Current behaviour: any joined singer (`server.ts:379`).

- **A — Any joined singer (ratify the status quo).** _Cost:_ zero code. Spends the possibility that
  someone's turn is ended by a person who is not singing, with nothing recorded about who did it.
- **B — Any joined singer, but the skip is attributed.** Add an actor field to the advance path and
  name them on the phone and the TV's transition. _Cost:_ one field on `advance()` plus copy on two
  surfaces. Spends no permission, and makes the open policy socially self-limiting.
- **C — The current singer alone, with a room-vote fallback.** Compare the actor against the playing
  entry's `singerId`; add a vote UI, a quorum rule and a tie rule for the case where the singer has
  put the phone down. _Cost:_ the largest of the five options — an entirely new interaction, and a
  stuck song whenever the singer stops holding their phone.
- **D — The current singer alone, no fallback.** _Cost:_ one comparison in `can()`'s call site. Spends
  the recoverability of a song nobody wants to finish; the only escape is the singer's own phone.

### Q2 — Who may move the guide-vocal and key controls while a song plays?

Current behaviour: any joined singer; last write wins; no attribution.

- **A — Any joined singer (ratify).** _Cost:_ zero. Spends the singer's control of their own
  performance — audibly, mid-song, to the whole room.
- **B — The singer of the playing entry only; everyone else sees a live read-only value.** _Cost:_ one
  actor comparison plus a disabled state on two controls. Needs no new identity concept —
  `currentEntryId` already resolves to a `singerId`. **Recommended in `XR-CON-02`.**
- **C — Any joined singer, with a short lock after each write.** First writer holds the control for N
  seconds; a second writer is refused with "Sam is adjusting this". _Cost:_ server-side lock state, a
  timer, and a new refusal string — plus a stated N no source can supply.
- **D — Any joined singer, but each phone keeps its own value locally.** _Cost:_ breaks the decision
  that these are shared live controls, and makes the TV's audio disagree with what a phone shows.
  Listed because it is the cheapest way to make the collision disappear, and to record that it does
  so by reversing a decision.

### Q3 — Should deployment-scoped settings be gated?

Current behaviour: any joined singer may `PUT /api/settings`; `settings.ts` says the scope is _"owned
by the operator"_.

- **A — Ratify open, and fix the doc comment to match.** _Cost:_ one comment. Spends the box's GPU
  budget to any guest who opens `/settings` and picks a slower engine mid-party.
- **B — Gate the four cost-changing fields behind an environment token.** `defaultEngine`,
  `defaultModel`, `upgradeEngine`, `enqueueUpgrade` require `ENCORE_HOST_TOKEN`; everything else stays
  open. _Cost:_ a token to configure and one branch in `can()`. Keeps "every joined singer is a host"
  true for the party surface. **Recommended in `XR-CON-03`.**
- **C — Gate all of `GlobalSettings` behind first-joiner-is-host.** _Cost:_ a host identity, a
  transfer rule for when the host's phone dies, and the first real asymmetry between guests — which
  `capabilities.ts` argues against on principle for a living-room LAN.
- **D — Move the cost-changing fields out of the guest-facing UI entirely** (env-var only, no HTTP
  write). _Cost:_ the host loses the ability to change engines without a restart; gains the smallest
  attack surface of the four, with no new identity concept at all.

### Q4 — Is a lost optimistic race disclosed, and how?

Current behaviour: disclosed. Rolled back to a pre-op snapshot, then `showError("Couldn't do that:
<raw validator string>")` in a `role="alert"` toast.

- **A — Ratify disclosure; replace raw validator strings with mapped copy.** _Cost:_ one message map
  over `validateCommand`'s return literals. **Recommended — this is `XR-CON-14`.**
- **B — Disclose only rejections the guest caused; revert the rest silently.** Distinguish
  "you can't do that" from "someone beat you to it" and show only the first. _Cost:_ a reason
  taxonomy on the wire, and a class of silent reverts a guest will read as the product ignoring them.
- **C — Disclose everything, including races, and name the other actor.** _Cost:_ the actor field from
  Q1 option B, plus copy for each race class. Highest information, most toasts.
- **D — Revert silently in all cases.** _Cost:_ delete the `onReject` wiring. Spends the guest's
  ability to distinguish a refusal from a bug — and `encore-ux-frame.md` §4 already records that a
  silent revert _"reads as the product having ignored them"_.

### Q5 — Is there a queue-depth limit per guest?

Current behaviour: none on queue depth. Process concurrency is capped at 3 with a 429 and real copy.

- **A — No depth limit; state that explicitly.** _Cost:_ one line of documentation. Round-robin already
  bounds the harm to list length rather than turn order. **Recommended in `XR-CON-05`.**
- **B — Cap queued-not-yet-started entries at N per guest.** _Cost:_ a count in `validateCommand`'s
  `add` arm, a refusal string, and a stated N no source can supply. Does not penalise a guest for
  songs they have already sung.
- **C — Cap total entries per guest at N.** _Cost:_ same code, simpler rule, but a prolific singer hits
  the cap because of their history rather than their pending queue — which reads as a punishment.
- **D — No cap, but reveal the shape.** Show each guest their own count and the room's total, and let
  round-robin plus visibility do the work. _Cost:_ copy on one surface, no refusal path at all.

I recommend ratifying Q4 option A and Q5 option A as written — they are documentation-and-copy
changes that close two of the five with no new concept — and deciding Q1, Q2 and Q3 together, since
all three turn on whether a host identity exists at all. `XR-CON-07` (arm-scoped wiring guards for
all five capabilities) should land **before** any of them, because it is what makes a narrowing
actually hold.
